\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Retrieval-augmented generation assumes that the retriever surfaces evidence that is valid
\emph{for the question being asked}. For temporally grounded questions---``Why did the number
of transactions per block jump in 2023?''---validity depends on time: a passage on the right
topic but about the wrong period is misleading evidence. Track~1 of the RETECO shared task
(SemEval-2027 Task~1) operationalises this with TEMPO \citep{abdallah2026tempo}, a 13-domain
benchmark of StackExchange questions whose relevant documents must be both topical and
temporally useful. The official BM25 baseline reaches only \rOfficialA{} (Sub-track~1a) and
\rOfficialB{} (1b) nDCG@10 on the development set.

A natural reading of the task is that systems should \emph{match time}: extract dates from
queries and documents, restrict or boost by period, and organise the corpus along a timeline.
We built and measured exactly these components---a year index, a temporal knowledge graph, a
temporal-alignment prior, temporal pseudo-relevance feedback, a time-aware RAPTOR tree, and
periods, events and entities that a small LLM extracts from every document and every
query---next to dense and reasoning-trained retrieval, LLM query planning and LLM reranking,
on the complete RETECO~v1.1 data (1.17M documents, all 13 domains, both sub-tracks). Two observations shape
the resulting system, \sys.

First, \textbf{surface temporal matching is already saturated}. Among queries that mention a
year, only \pctGoldOverlap{} of gold documents mention a year within $\pm1$ of it, against
\pctTopOverlap{} of the non-relevant documents that BM25 ranks in its top~10
(Table~\ref{tab:data}): lexical retrieval already over-rewards matching dates. Consistently,
year-based priors, filters and graphs do not improve, and often hurt, a lexical first stage,
and this does not change when the periods are inferred by an LLM rather than by rules.
Second, \textbf{temporal relevance is mostly semantic}. Passage-level BM25 alone lifts the
official baseline from \rOfficialA{} to \rBmA{} (1a), and an instruction-tuned dense encoder
reaches \rDenseA{}, because relevant evidence describes the events and mechanisms behind a
question rather than repeating its dates.

\sys therefore uses time where it carries information that text similarity does not---in
LLM-generated \emph{neighbouring events} (causes, earlier and later events, each with a year)
that become sub-queries, in the reasoning that a reasoning-trained retriever receives, in the
rendering of candidates and in the instructions of LLM rerankers---and spends its capacity on
a hybrid first stage selected on train and on LLM reranking. On the dev split it reaches
\rFinalA{} nDCG@10 on 1a and \rFinalB{} on 1b, \gainOfficialA\% and \gainOfficialB\% above the
official baseline. Because RETECO's train and dev questions are exactly the 1{,}730 questions
of the TEMPO benchmark, we also place \sys on the TEMPO leaderboard: with \pooledFinalA{}
(1a) and \pooledFinalB{} (1b) nDCG@10$\times$100 it is above all twelve published retrievers
on questions and on par with the best ones on steps, but below ReasonIR given GPT-4o's
reasoning (41.0; Table~\ref{tab:tempo}).

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A controlled comparison, on all 13 domains and both sub-tracks, of temporal structures
        (year index, temporal KG, temporal prior, temporal PRF, temporal RAPTOR, and
        LLM-extracted document and query periods used as filters, priors and a metadata
        index), lexical and LLM query expansion (RM3, keywords, query2doc, multi-query with
        an LLM-generated \emph{event chain}, neighbouring events, and LLM-based
        pseudo-relevance feedback), dense and reasoning-trained retrieval (ReasonIR-8B with
        and without LLM reasoning), and encoder and decoder rerankers, with every
        configuration selected on train.
  \item A diagnostic of TEMPO showing why date matching fails as a relevance signal, and the
        finding that passage-level retrieval alone explains most of the gap to the official
        baseline.
  \item A comparison with the TEMPO leaderboard on the same questions and steps.
  \item A head-to-head comparison of encoder cross-encoders and decoder LLM rerankers
        (``cross-decoders'') for selecting the top-10 out of the same top-100.
  \item An open, staged implementation that runs on a laptop CPU and on a GPU cluster, with
        cached stages, official scoring and significance tests.\footnote{Code:
        \url{https://github.com/ITU-NLP-Team/nlp-term-project}}
\end{itemize}
