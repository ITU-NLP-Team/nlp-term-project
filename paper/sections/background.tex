\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Task.} Track~1 of RETECO is built on TEMPO \citep{abdallah2026tempo}: StackExchange
questions from 13 domains (blockchain, social sciences, applied and STEM sites), each domain
with its own corpus (1{,}167{,}159 documents in v1.1, after deduplication). In Sub-track~1a a
system ranks documents for the whole question; in Sub-track~1b it ranks documents for each
step of an annotated reasoning decomposition. Relevance is binary and requires a passage to be
both topical and temporally useful. The metric is nDCG@10, averaged per domain and
macro-averaged over domains. The released train/dev splits are 70/30 per domain; the test set
is hidden. For train/dev queries, TEMPO also releases \emph{query guidance}---temporal intent,
expected granularity, time anchors, temporal events and a step-wise retrieval plan---which is
not available at test time.

\paragraph{Lexical and dense retrieval.} BM25 \citep{robertson2009bm25} remains a strong
first stage; passage-level scoring with MaxP \citep{dai2019maxp} handles long documents.
Pseudo-relevance feedback (RM3; \citealp{lavrenko2001relevance,abduljaleel2004umass}) expands a
query with terms from its top results. Dense bi-encoders \citep{karpukhin2020dpr,xiao2023cpack}
and recent LLM-based embedders \citep{zhang2025qwen3emb} capture semantic similarity;
reciprocal rank fusion (RRF; \citealp{cormack2009rrf}) is a robust way to combine rankings.
Reasoning-intensive benchmarks such as BRIGHT \citep{su2025bright} show that both families
struggle when relevance requires inference, which motivated reasoning-trained retrievers
\citep{shao2025reasonir}.

\paragraph{LLMs for retrieval.} LLMs rewrite queries by generating hypothetical documents
(HyDE, \citealp{gao2023hyde}; query2doc, \citealp{wang2023query2doc}) or multiple sub-queries
whose results are fused \citep{rackauckas2024ragfusion}. For reranking, encoder
cross-encoders \citep{nogueira2019passage} were followed by sequence-to-sequence
\citep{nogueira2020monot5} and decoder-only pointwise rerankers \citep{zhang2025qwen3emb}, and
by listwise prompting with sliding windows \citep{sun2023rankgpt}.

\paragraph{Structure-aware retrieval.} RAPTOR \citep{sarthi2024raptor} builds a tree of
recursive cluster summaries; GraphRAG \citep{edge2024graphrag} and HippoRAG
\citep{gutierrez2024hipporag} retrieve through entity graphs, the latter with personalised
PageRank. Cluster-based retrieval in IR \citep{liu2004cluster,kurland2004corpus} smooths a
document's score with that of its cluster.

\paragraph{Temporal IR.} Temporal expression taggers \citep{strotgen2010heideltime} normalise
dates; time-aware ranking combines textual relevance with a temporal similarity between query
and document \citep{campos2014survey}, and the temporal profile of a query can be estimated from
the dates of its top-ranked documents \citep{jones2007temporal}. TEMPO's analysis reports that
lexical, dense and reasoning retrievers all stay far below their performance on non-temporal
benchmarks \citep{abdallah2026tempo}; we test which of the above ideas transfer.
