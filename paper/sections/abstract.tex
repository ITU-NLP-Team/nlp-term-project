\begin{abstract}
Temporal reasoning-intensive retrieval asks for evidence that is not only on topic but useful
for the period and events a question depends on. We describe \sys, our system for both
sub-tracks of RETECO Track~1 (SemEval-2027 Task~1), and use it to test, on all 13 domains and
1.17M documents, which retrieval ideas transfer to this setting: date-based structures (a year
index, a temporal knowledge graph, temporal priors and temporal pseudo-relevance feedback),
periods, events and entities extracted from every document and query by a small LLM, a
time-aware RAPTOR tree, LLM query planning with event chains, neighbouring events, multi-query,
query2doc and LLM-based feedback, dense and reasoning-trained retrievers, and encoder vs.\
decoder rerankers that select the top-10 from a top-100. Matching dates does not help, even
when the dates are inferred by an LLM: gold documents overlap the query's years \emph{less}
often than the non-relevant documents BM25 ranks highly. What helps is retrieval granularity
(passage-level BM25 lifts the official baseline from \rOfficialA{} to \rBmA{} nDCG@10),
semantic retrieval (\rDenseA{}) and \AbstractRerank{} With every choice made on train, \sys
reaches \rFinalA{} (1a) and \rFinalB{} (1b) nDCG@10 on dev, \gainOfficialA\% and
\gainOfficialB\% above the official baseline; on all 1{,}730 TEMPO questions it scores
\pooledFinalA{} ($\times$100), above the best retriever on the TEMPO leaderboard (32.0) but
below a reasoning retriever given GPT-4o's reasoning (41.0).
\end{abstract}
